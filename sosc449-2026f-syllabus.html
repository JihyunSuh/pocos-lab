Course description

Cognitive neuroscience studies the neural mechanisms underlying human cognition. It is an interdisciplinary field that integrates psychology, neurology, life sciences, computer science, mathematics, and chemistry. In this course you will learn about the structural and functional mechanisms of the brain related to perception, consciousness, memory, attention, language, decision making, and emotion. You will also become familiar with the research methods used in cognitive neuroscience (experimental studies, neuroimaging techniques, computational modeling, and others) and develop the ability to critically analyze recent research findings.

Grading criteria

Attendance (10%)

  • The first two absences are not counted against the attendance score.
  • Each additional absence (after the two allowed) deducts 1 point.
  • Three late arrivals (15 minutes or more) count as one absence. Arriving 30 minutes or more late counts as an absence.
  • Absences for medical reasons or official duties may be excused if you notify the instructor by email in advance and submit supporting documents afterward.
  • Attendance requires staying for the entire class session. Signing in and then leaving early without prior permission is treated as an absence.
  • Five absences result in a grade of F.
  • Attendance is counted starting from the second week of the semester.

Exams (50%)

  • Exams consist of multiple-choice and short-answer questions.
  • Students who cannot attend an exam for personal reasons must obtain permission from the instructor in advance and submit supporting documents afterward.

Paper (20%)

  • Select a recent research article related to a topic covered in class and submit a paper describing its theoretical background, purpose, methods, results, interpretation, limitations, and suggestions for future research.

Presentation (10%)

  • Present the content of your paper (approximately 5–10 minutes).

Questions (10%)

  • Post specific, academic questions related to the course content on the PLMS board.
  • At least five questions must be posted over the semester, with a maximum of one question per class session.
  • Questions that lack academic value or specificity will not be counted (e.g., “I am curious about functional connectivity in the brain”).

Academic integrity

All work submitted in this course must be your own. Cheating on exams, plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and submitting work previously used in another course are violations of academic integrity.

  • Plagiarism includes using another person’s words, ideas, figures, or data without proper citation. Cite all sources in your paper and presentation, including sources you paraphrase.
  • Violations will be reported to the university and may result in a zero on the assignment or exam and a failing grade for the course, in addition to any sanction imposed by the university.
  • If you are unsure whether a particular practice is acceptable, ask the instructor before submitting.

Use of large language models (LLMs)

  • You may use LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and similar tools) to support your work in this course — for example, to clarify concepts, find related literature, check grammar, or get feedback on a draft.
  • Copying and pasting LLM output into your paper or presentation is not acceptable. Any material you take from an LLM must be rewritten in your own words and reorganized to fit your own argument.
  • You are responsible for everything you submit. LLMs produce plausible but incorrect statements and fabricated citations, so verify every claim and reference against the original source. Errors introduced by an LLM will be graded as your errors, and a fabricated citation is treated as fabrication of data.

Students with disabilities

Any student who needs accommodations to participate fully in this course is encouraged to contact the instructor. Available accommodations include:

  • For coursework: text interpretation / captioning (hearing impairment), course assistants (developmental disability), note-taking support (all types).
  • For exams: extended exam time (all types, as needed), enlarged copies of exam materials (visual impairment).
  • For any additional request, contact the Support Center for Students with Disabilities at 279-2434.
  • All discussions regarding accommodations will be kept confidential.
