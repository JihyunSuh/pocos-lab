Course description

Cognitive psychology is a branch of psychology that scientifically studies the foundations of the human mind and behavior. In this course, we will explore and discuss a variety of experimental studies that investigate human cognitive processes. Key topics include visual information processing, attention, memory, language, decision-making, and consciousness, along with recent findings from research in brain and cognitive sciences.

Course objectives

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Develop critical thinking skills – Evaluate empirical findings in cognitive psychology by assessing research methods, evidence, and conclusions.
  2. Build communication skills – Practice explaining and summarizing research through writing, peer feedback, and responses to academic articles.
  3. Apply knowledge to real-world issues – Connect theories and findings to real-world problems, current issues in psychology, and everyday behavior.

Key topics

  • Core research designs – How cognitive psychologists use experimental, behavioral, and neuroscience methods to study mental processes
  • Cognitive neuroscience – Brain mechanisms underlying cognition, explored through neuroimaging and other tools
  • Perception & visual information processing – How we interpret sensory input and construct our experience of the world
  • Attention – How we focus on relevant information while filtering out distractions
  • Memory – Short-term, working, and long-term memory processes, including encoding, storage, and retrieval
  • Language – How we understand, produce, and acquire language
  • Learning & knowledge representation – How we acquire new information and organize it mentally
  • Decision-making & problem-solving – How we reason, make choices, and solve complex tasks, including biases and heuristics
  • Consciousness & metacognition – Awareness of thoughts, self-monitoring, and higher-order thinking processes

Exams (60%)

Exams consist of multiple-choice and short-answer questions.

Team project (30%)

Students will form teams to conduct an experimental research project. Each team will select one of the options below and carry out the study throughout the semester, then give a final presentation at the end of the course. Team members may participate in each other’s studies if needed. After the presentation, each team will submit a written report (details will be announced later). The topic must be finalized through a meeting with the instructor.

  1. Replication study – Choose an existing study of interest and attempt to replicate it.
  2. Original study – Conduct a new research project related to cognitive psychology.
